The workbook contains **84 grouped etymological analyses**, covering **191 distinct
current prompt–form pairs** from **376 responses by nine released speakers** and
the previously released historical material. Its archive sheet has 93 recording
rows: 55 recordings outside the current selection and 38 current recordings with
additional transcription layers or derived-unit references. These overlap the
current response count and must not be added to it.

This GitHub edition includes only contributions already released in this
repository. Website-only and withheld contributions are excluded. Local filesystem
paths have been replaced with bibliographic references, entry/page locators and
public links. Reference books and local research copies are not bundled.

## Reading the analyses

Start with the workbook's **Roots** sheet. **Forms** maps each distinct released
form to its analysis. **Published responses** preserves source utterances,
selected comparison forms, IPA and accepted alternatives as separate fields.
**Published archive** retains older, retired, derived and supplemental evidence,
with intended prompts and documented actual meanings kept separate. **Sources**
records what was consulted and the limits of that evidence.

Historical confidence is a qualitative research judgment at the stated depth:

| Confidence | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| High | A strong sourced connection at the stated historical depth. A deeper reconstruction can remain uncertain. |
| Medium | A plausible sourced comparison with unresolved sound history, derivation or borrowing route. |
| Low | A tentative lead with substantial unresolved questions. |
| Unresolved | No defensible historical root selected. |

The 84 analyses comprise 38 High, 24 Medium, nine Low and 13 Unresolved judgments.
These are counts of grouped analyses, not independent recordings or statistical
probabilities. Confidence in a historical family does not approve a transcription,
settle the intended meaning or establish comparable verb tense and grammar.
Published provisional and comparison-excluded responses retain those labels.

An asterisk marks a reconstructed form. PIE means Proto-Indo-European. Ancient
and neighboring languages provide comparative evidence; they are not automatically
direct ancestors or borrowing donors. Dictionary matches establish forms and
meanings but do not alone prove historical descent. Sources cited indirectly
through IE-CoR annotations are distinguished from independently consulted works.

These assistant-prepared analyses are proposals for review. They do not change
the canonical survey transcriptions, comparison annotations, empty cognate/borrowing
columns, consent boundaries or acoustic-training eligibility. Exact published
spellings and IPA have been retained. The tables and workbook share the same
analysis and source IDs so that uncertain proposals can be discussed and corrected.
